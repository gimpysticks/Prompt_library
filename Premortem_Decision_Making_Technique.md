# Premortem Decision-Making Technique

## OCR Transcription

### 1. Origins

Psychologist Gary Klein created a fix for this and published it in *Harvard Business Review*.

Daniel Kahneman called it his most valuable decision-making technique, and companies like Google, Goldman Sachs, and Procter & Gamble use it before major launches.

---

### 2. What is a Premortem?

It's called a **premortem**, and it's the opposite of a postmortem.

Instead of analyzing failure after it happens, imagine the project has already failed and determine why **before** you begin.

---

### 3. Why It Works

Researchers at Wharton and Cornell called this **prospective hindsight**.

Instead of asking:

> What could go wrong?

Ask:

> This project already failed. Tell me why.

This framing produces more specific, creative, and honest explanations.

---

### 4. Why It Works Well with AI

Rather than asking:

> Is this a good plan?

Use:

> This already failed. Explain why.

That encourages the AI to focus on weaknesses instead of simply validating the idea.

---

### 5. What You Receive

A strong premortem should identify:

- Realistic failure modes
- Chains of events leading to failure
- Hidden assumptions
- Early warning signs

---

### 6. Strategic Breakdown

The analysis should answer:

- Which failure is most likely?
- Which failure is most dangerous?
- What hidden assumption matters most?
- What changes strengthen the plan?
- What checklist should be completed before launch?

---

### 7. Hidden Assumptions

Hidden assumptions are often the most valuable discovery.

They are frequently so obvious they become invisible, yet the success of the entire plan depends on them.

Finding them early can save months of wasted effort.

---

### 8. Simple Prompt

Give the AI your plan and say:

> **Premortem this.**

Instead of asking the AI to validate the idea, ask it to break the plan so you can rebuild it stronger.

---

### 9. Takeaway

The best decisions are not made by people who avoid failure.

They are made by people who prepare for failure before it happens.

Gary Klein knew it.
Daniel Kahneman knew it.
Many organizations use the technique before important launches.
